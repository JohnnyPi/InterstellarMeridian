## 1. Purpose and authority

This document describes the civilizations and institutions that give the persistent universe its identity. It is a content reference for D6 (economy and faction simulation), not a substitute for its algorithms or technical contracts. D0 and D1 govern product and engineering requirements. D6B governs established historical facts where earlier proposals conflicted. This document is an original setting reference for the independent project and does not amend those requirements.

**Established by the owner:** seven government names below and their previously discussed political concepts; approximately three to five diversified competing megacorporations; independent mining and industrial unions; contrasting pirate cultures; a universe-wide Academy attempting neutrality; familiar but futuristic religious and ideological institutions; neutral authorities; persistent supply lines; rank-based command; player investment without station/factory construction; pilot-first progression with multi-capital-ship fleets; component upgrades, tuning stamps and attachable specialized parts.

**Provisionally retained:** other faction names from the brainstorming roster. Except where explicitly marked established, the detailed histories, leaders, institutions, ranks, laws, relationships, equipment and events below remain proposals carried forward from v0.1. No fixed calendar, detailed species roster, canonical territorial map, new starting war or simulation scale is established here. The established historical war and fragile peace are setting facts, not a requirement for a scripted campaign.

## 2. Political world model

Organizations overlap rather than partition the universe into exclusive map colors. Distinguish sovereign jurisdiction, installation ownership, ship ownership, operator, citizenship, employment, membership, belief, rank, and reputation. A Concordancy citizen can work for Vanguard, belong to the Celestial Continuum and serve as an Academy field associate. A corporate station inside a sovereign system may have contractual privileges but not automatic immunity from local law.

| Organization | Principal source of power | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Law, taxation, legitimacy, coercion | Territory, fleets, public services |
| Megacorporation | Capital, production, distribution | Factories, mines, yards, freighters, security fleets |
| Union | Collective bargaining and productive capacity | Member operations, depots, contracts |
| Pirate faction | Mobility, force, secrecy, networks | Ships, hidden bases, captured supplies |
| Academy | Expertise and shared scientific trust | Laboratories, observatories, survey fleets |
| Religion | Belief, community, service | Temples, charities, research or pilgrimage sites |
| Ideological movement | Public influence and advocacy | Networks, funding, publications |
| Neutral authority | Recognized agreements and useful services | Registries, offices, service vessels |

No organization is omnipotent or homogeneous. Distinguish declared policy from actual practice, and leadership from members. Neutrality is an aspiration enforced through agreements, not immunity. Organizations can split, reform, lose assets or change alliances; procedural change must leave comprehensible history.

## 3. Historical foundations and confidence levels

### 3.1 Established setting (D6B §2 and §4)

Humanity originated on Earth and settled another galaxy. Seven governments descend from distinct generation-ship expeditions launched by different Earth factions at different times. Earth is not an ordinary accessible destination. Interstellar travel is established within the adopted galaxy; intergalactic travel requires generation ships or extraordinary energy. Exact propulsion and travel times remain open.

Contemporary civilizations are of human origin; other lifeforms exist, and remains survive from civilizations that destroyed themselves or disappeared. There are no known contemporary extraterrestrial civilizations. Artificial intelligence, cybernetics, genetic engineering and synthetic consciousness are accepted setting topics, but their prevalence, legal status and mechanics remain open.

Bastion's founding experience includes an onboard emergency, premature awakening of part of the cryogenic population, repair needs, loss of many pods and conflict among the Awakened over scarce supplies. A later vast war devastated settled space; the seven governments now largely coexist in fragile peace, with some friendly relationships. The setting has a 5,000-year history; counting from first settlement is a working interpretation, not a fixed calendar.

### 3.2 Candidate historical development

The following relative eras preserve useful earlier proposals as possible developments within the established setting, not a fixed chronology. D6B supplies optional chronology and expedition heritage. The labels Great Fracture and Interstellar Accords are working names only.

| Era | Proposed development | Present-day implications |
|---|---|---|
| Exodus and settlement | Distinct expeditions arrive and establish settlements; founding vessels have differing fates | Shared human origins; divergent institutions |
| Expansion | Transport consortia and specialized habitats support growth | Shared navigation standards; divergent local cultures |
| Separation and institutional development | Distance and uneven resources reinforce local institutions | Republics, hereditary stewardship, autonomous habitats |
| Consolidation | Industrial mergers create four vertically integrated giants | Cross-border production and concentrated market power |
| War and disruption | Supply failures, displacement and conflict reshape existing governments and institutions | Renegade fleets, bargaining institutions, damaged infrastructure; Bastion's origin predates this war |
| Postwar agreements | Rival powers negotiate minimum standards for trade, science and rescue | Neutral agencies with uneven treaty coverage; possible prewar roots |
| Present | A fragile interconnected order competes over resources, technology and legitimacy | Player enters a universe already in motion |

The order of arrivals, founding vessel names and fates, specific Earth factions, war causes and alliances, exact calendar and precursor identities remain open. Religious and scientific claims are beliefs or research agendas, not confirmation of setting facts.

## 4. Sovereign governments

The following seven names and broad political concepts are established direction. Detailed profiles are proposals. Government rank examples describe faction-specific authority rather than universal character levels.

### 4.1 The Concordancy

**Identity:** Constitutional representative democracy; liberty through unity.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: a coalition of democratic nations and international institutions preserving a charter. A later compact of colonies rejecting emergency rule could reinforce that tradition.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Federal Assembly, First Minister, independent courts; elections and delegated local administration. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Civil libertarians, social reformers and security interventionists dispute surveillance and military commitments. Public hearings and civic service are prestigious. |
| Rights and restrictions | Due process, open commerce under regulation, protected dissent; warrants and oversight constrain searches, with emergency exceptions subject to review. |
| Economy and dependencies | Diversified markets, skilled services and trade hubs; depends on industrial imports, stable freight and public revenue. |
| Military doctrine | Concordancy Defense Force: professional mixed fleets, convoy protection, coalition operations; civilian authorization for campaigns. |
| Membership and authority | Concordants; recruit, lieutenant, captain, admiral; civic service and diplomatic appointments separate from military rank. |
| External relationships | Choral shares representative ideals; Bastion disputes security policy; Meridian is a major supplier but subject to competition law. |
| Player opportunities | Escort a threatened trade lane, investigate corruption, negotiate treaty access, or serve in a fleet under changing elected priorities. |
| Visual language | Blue, silver, ice cyan; civic plazas, readable signage, functional ships. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | A panic after attacks may empower security advocates; elections can change deployments and trade rules. |

### 4.2 Albion

**Identity:** Neo-feudal aristocracy; honor and stewardship.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: a privately financed expedition whose shipboard custodianship developed into hereditary stewardship. Later habitat protectors and fleet families could convert inherited responsibilities into dynastic claims.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Council of Houses and High Sovereign; regional estates administer local law; succession depends on recognized claims. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Traditionalists, reformist houses, ambitious cadet branches and commoner officers compete. Oaths, patronage and hospitality are important. |
| Rights and restrictions | House privileges, layered jurisdiction and obligations of protection; access to noble estates may require sponsorship. |
| Economy and dependencies | Estate revenues, luxury trade, agricultural and mineral concessions; imports advanced machinery and fleet components. |
| Military doctrine | Royal Astral Navy plus house squadrons; heavy capital ships, retainers and formal battle groups; divided loyalties can complicate command. |
| Membership and authority | Albionites; sworn pilot, knight-captain, banner commander, fleet marshal; titles and naval billets are distinct. |
| External relationships | Vanguard supplies heavy fleets; Choral challenges hereditary legitimacy; Corsairs may receive letters of marque; Church maintains ceremonial ties. |
| Player opportunities | Escort an heir, defend a vassal convoy, resolve succession claims, earn a commission or choose between rival houses. |
| Visual language | Burgundy, gold, ivory; heraldry, ornate bridges, monumental stations. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | A disputed succession can redirect house fleets and produce competing claims without all Albion becoming hostile. |

### 4.3 Codex

**Identity:** Algorithmic technocracy; competence and evidence.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: a scientific and engineering coalition using evidence-based resource allocation. Technical administrators could later gain legitimacy by restoring failing habitat infrastructure through coordinated planning.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Directorate, Prime Architect, expert councils; human officials approve policy supported by computational forecasts. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Empiricists, optimization hardliners and human-centered critics disagree about metrics, oversight and individual choice. |
| Rights and restrictions | Competency credentials and audited decisions; broad technical access for accredited workers; restrictions on hazardous experimentation and contested algorithmic appeals. |
| Economy and dependencies | High-precision industry, information services, efficient logistics; relies on rare materials and diverse research inputs. |
| Military doctrine | Codex Strategic Command: reconnaissance, coordination, electronic warfare and carefully allocated forces; vulnerable to incomplete intelligence. |
| Membership and authority | Codicians; candidate, accredited specialist, unit lead, strategic architect; military command separately certified. |
| External relationships | Academy collaboration but disputes over openness; Asterion supplies precision equipment; Ascendant Path attracts some technologists. |
| Player opportunities | Survey routes, test prediction failures, expose manipulated data, escort research or serve as a tactical coordinator. |
| Visual language | Deep blue, luminous cyan, metallic gray; geometric infrastructure and precise displays. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | A flawed model can trigger misallocation, public inquiry and reform; reliable evidence can overturn a policy. |

### 4.4 The Gold League

**Identity:** Contractual shareholder government; prosperity and negotiated obligation.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: a commercial consortium governing passage, duties and rights through contracts. Settlements could later pool security and infrastructure under a charter of contractual citizenship.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Board of Delegates and Executive Chancellor; representation mixes residency and economic participation under local charters. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Market liberals, entrenched investors and citizen-rights reformers disagree over access and concentration. Contract ritual and commercial hospitality dominate. |
| Rights and restrictions | Contract enforcement, commercial arbitration, transferable residency under conditions; unequal ability to purchase services is a central tension. |
| Economy and dependencies | Finance, distribution, insurance, free ports; depends on investor confidence and functioning shipping. |
| Military doctrine | League Security Fleet and contracted forces: protect assets, intercept threats, prioritize contractual commitments. |
| Membership and authority | Leaguers; licensed operator, contract officer, security captain, executive commander; wealth does not itself grant a military billet. |
| External relationships | All four corporations operate here; unions contest bargaining power; arbitration and registry services are valuable. |
| Player opportunities | Negotiate freight guarantees, investigate fraud, escort insured cargo or defend a station in which the player has invested. |
| Visual language | Charcoal, gold, warm amber; branded terminals, polished commercial hubs. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | A market crash can change voting coalitions, security budgets and contract enforcement. |

### 4.5 Choral

**Identity:** Decentralized cooperative socialism; shared welfare and solidarity.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: labor, agricultural and ecological cooperatives sharing essential resources. Later worker-managed settlements could coordinate mutual aid after exploitative concession agreements collapsed.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Grand Chorus and First Voice; local assemblies send accountable delegates and negotiate shared policy. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Local autonomy advocates, central planners and pragmatic trade delegates disagree on resource allocation and emergency powers. |
| Rights and restrictions | Collective ownership of key infrastructure, participatory governance, labor protections; private personal ships remain possible under local rules. |
| Economy and dependencies | Cooperative mining, agriculture, manufacturing and public services; depends on consensus, imports and trade partners. |
| Military doctrine | Choral Collective Defense: pooled ships, militia reserves and elected or appointed operational officers; delegated battlefield authority avoids voting on every maneuver. |
| Membership and authority | Choralians; volunteer, formation delegate, operations commander, defense coordinator. |
| External relationships | Strong union relationships; ideological tension with Albion and Gold League; practical trade with corporations. |
| Player opportunities | Deliver supplies to a cooperative, mediate allocation disputes, lead a defense formation or break a blockade. |
| Visual language | Green, cream, mint; communal gardens, modular habitats, shared civic spaces. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | A shortage tests whether communities share resources or prioritize local needs; emergency mandates can provoke backlash. |

### 4.6 Bastion

**Identity:** Militarized security state; survival through readiness.

**Founding tradition:** Established: an onboard emergency prematurely awakened part of the cryogenically suspended population; repairs were needed, many pods were lost, and the Awakened fought over limited supplies. Proposed development: centralized emergency authority and permanent defense institutions arose from these conditions. The exact failure, duration, casualties and institutional transition remain open (D6B §4).

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Security Council and High Marshal; civilian departments operate within strategic priorities. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Hardliners, professional officers and civilian reformers dispute whether emergency restrictions remain necessary. Service and preparedness confer prestige. |
| Rights and restrictions | Mandatory public service proposed; strict security checks, controlled strategic exports, limited dissent and clear military jurisdiction. |
| Economy and dependencies | Resilient infrastructure, reserves, defense manufacturing; costly mobilization relies on fuel, industry and reliable supply. |
| Military doctrine | Bastion Armed Forces: integrated capital fleets, layered defense, logistics and readiness doctrine. |
| Membership and authority | Bastionites; recruit, lieutenant, captain, commodore, marshal; promotions depend on service and authorized billets. |
| External relationships | Vanguard is a natural supplier; Broken Standard may be a historic adversary; Concordancy challenges restrictions. |
| Player opportunities | Escort military tenders, patrol civilian lanes, expose false threat assessments, or rise through fleet command. |
| Visual language | Gunmetal, crimson, white; fortified stations, utilitarian hulls and clear hazard markings. |
| Dynamic pressure and response | An attack may justify redeploying patrols, opening civilian lanes to piracy; prolonged peace could empower reformers. |

### 4.7 The Rhizome

**Identity:** Confederation of autonomous settlements; local sovereignty.

**Founding tradition:** Proposed expedition heritage: an alliance of independent communities with modular autonomous habitats. Later habitats could form mutual-defense and transit agreements without surrendering local self-rule.

| Dimension | Proposed profile |
|---|---|
| Governance | Confluence and Rotating Convenor; agreements bind participating settlements, not automatically every member. |
| Culture and internal divisions | Mutualists, local separatists, commercial free-port advocates and defense integrationists disagree on common obligations. |
| Rights and restrictions | Highly local rules; shared agreements protect transit and arbitration where ratified; entry and enforcement vary by habitat. |
| Economy and dependencies | Distributed workshops, salvage, commerce and independent services; relies on coordination and cross-border supply. |

| Membership and authority | Rhizomites; local pilot, elected wing lead, task-group coordinator; authority limited by mandate. |
| External relationships | Unbound share some autonomy ideals but raids divide opinion; unions and neutral agencies provide useful coordination. |

## 5. Megacorporations

Four integrated giants compete across all major product categories: ships, weapons, shields, drives, electronics, consumer goods, medical supplies, extraction and manufacturing. Each maintains its own mining and industrial subsidiaries. Their differences are tendencies, not hard restrictions or guaranteed item bonuses. Independent production remains important.

### 5.1 Asterion Dynamics

*Precision. Performance. Superiority.* Founded provisionally through mergers of specialist aerospace and instrumentation firms. A board and chief executive oversee research, production, distribution and security divisions. Engineering prestige and patent ownership dominate its culture; open-research advocates oppose exclusive licensing. Premium lightweight hulls, accurate weapons, high-output drives and highly tunable components are its signature. It depends on rare materials, skilled staff and sophisticated suppliers. Its security doctrine emphasizes intelligence and precise interventions rather than attrition. Novagen is its technological rival; the Academy and Industrial Union contest proprietary restrictions. Player careers include prototype testing, research escort, intelligence recovery and accredited equipment service. Progression: contractor → authorized specialist → project lead → division officer, with separate security command. Navy blue, ice cyan and silver; clean angular designs. A patent leak may trigger litigation, covert acquisition, cheaper competing parts or a security operation.

### 5.2 Meridian Consolidated

*Everything. Everywhere.* A proposed consolidation of freight networks, retail chains and standardized manufacturers. Regional directors answer to a central board; logistics executives compete with cost-cutting and labor-relations factions. Its culture rewards reliability, volume and predictable delivery. Standardized ships, efficient drives, accessible repair networks and inexpensive everyday goods distinguish it. Its greatest dependency is uninterrupted high-volume freight. Security prioritizes escorts, traffic intelligence and protection of distribution hubs. The Miners' Union is both supplier and bargaining adversary; the Unbound target its monopoly practices. Player careers include freight, distribution contracts, convoy command and investment. Progression: approved carrier → route contractor → network coordinator → regional director. Amber, slate and cream; modular standardized geometry. A port closure forces rerouting and creates shortages across many systems.

### 5.3 Vanguard Industries

*Built to endure.* Proposed origins in heavy construction and military procurement. Its board oversees foundries, shipyards, extraction concessions and a disciplined security organization. Engineers favor reliability while expansionist executives pursue strategic contracts. Rugged hulls, durable engines, heavy weapons and high-capacity shields are characteristic. It depends on bulk ore, refined alloys, energy and large customers. Its security fleets can field multiple capital ships; open conflict remains expensive and politically consequential. Albion and Bastion are potential customers, not permanent allies. Industrial Union yards compete for repair contracts. Player careers include industrial haulage, security service, testing and combat logistics. Progression: contractor → certified operator → security officer or project supervisor → task-group or division director. Crimson, charcoal and steel; industrial armor and visible structural ribs. Loss of a foundry or military contract can alter output and fleet readiness.

### 5.4 Novagen Universal

*Tomorrow, today.* Proposed merger of experimental propulsion, materials and medical research businesses. Research directors and commercial executives compete over safety, secrecy and time to market. Its culture prizes novelty, sometimes at the expense of caution. Adaptive shields, exotic sensors, unusual drive systems and specialized parts are characteristic, with ordinary mainstream products also available. It depends on discoveries, laboratory capacity, rare inputs and public trust. Security protects research shipments and installations; it contracts additional force as required. The Academy is both partner and critic; Asterion is a research rival. Player careers include anomaly surveys, sample transport, prototype testing and investigation of failed experiments. Progression: field contractor → research partner → program lead → research director. Jade, violet and white; flowing forms and luminous instrumentation. A failed trial can produce recalls, shortages, investigations and competing bids for recovered technology.

### 5.5 Competition and player-facing equipment

| Field | Asterion | Meridian | Vanguard | Novagen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drives | Performance | Efficiency | Durability | Experimental mechanisms |
| Shields | Regulation | Affordability | Capacity | Adaptive behavior |
| Weapons | Precision | Standardization | Heavy output | Unusual effects |
| Hulls | Lightweight | Economical | Reinforced | Advanced composites |
| Sensors | Resolution | General purpose | Hardened | Anomaly detection |

Conflict can take the form of bidding, price wars, lobbying, patent disputes, espionage, proxy raids, embargoes and open military engagements. Escalation requires objectives, resources, opportunities and political consequences. A corporate action inside another government's territory is subject to jurisdiction and negotiated privileges. Manufacturer and operator identities remain separate. A player can mix compatible equipment brands, save named tuning recipes, and attach acquired specialized parts; no recipe duplicates physical items. Corporate investment grants financial interests, not automatic command or sovereignty.

## 6. Independent unions

### 6.1 Independent Miners' Union (IMU)

Origin proposal: independent prospectors organized after concession holders imposed unfavorable purchasing terms. A congress of local chapters elects negotiators; cooperatives retain ownership of their assets. Moderates seek reliable corporate contracts while militant members favor strikes and collective bargaining. It maintains claim records, survey exchanges, depots, armed mining ships and contracted escorts. It relies on buyers, repair yards and safe freight. The Industrial Union is a natural partner; every megacorporation can be customer or adversary. Membership could progress from registered prospector to chapter delegate and regional negotiator; defense authority is separately delegated. Player work: prospecting, extraction, claim verification, convoy protection, mediation and investment in existing operations. Industrial orange, ochre and cream; rugged equipment and practical markings. A strike should actually reduce production or deliveries and affect member income, not merely trigger a news headline.

### 6.2 United Industrial Union (UIU)

Origin proposal: independent workshops and worker associations formed a mutual purchasing and certification network to resist proprietary supply dependence. A federated council represents yards, factories and engineering cooperatives. Open-standard advocates and licensed manufacturers disagree on intellectual property. It operates refineries, repair yards, factories and shipyards through member organizations. Defense is contracted or locally organized. It depends on ore, energy, skilled labor, customers and replacement parts. It trades with all four corporations while competing with them. Progression: accredited technician → member operator → chapter representative → industrial coordinator. Player work: salvage, component tuning, spare-part delivery, industrial investment and ship refits. Teal, steel and white; utilitarian modular facilities. A shortage of specialized parts can shift repair demand toward refurbished equipment and generate recovery contracts.

## 7. Pirate organizations

Piracy is a legal classification and mode of operation, not a universal ideology. These six factions are candidate organizations, not all required at launch. They have finite supplies, buyers, repair capacity and refuge. A pirate's known actions and jurisdiction determine legal consequences; ideology alone does not prove a crime.

| Organization | Origin and culture | Authority and internal tension | Economy and fleet doctrine | Relations and player opportunities | Visual identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Crimson Maw | Predatory raider crews united by fear; glorify cruelty and domination | Strong captains, challengers, coerced followers; unstable succession | Loot, extortion and captured supplies; aggressive raiding, limited durable logistics | Widely opposed; rescue captives, defend lanes, pursue bounties or infiltrate; negotiations unreliable | Black, crimson, jagged markings |
| The Unbound | Radicals rejecting state and corporate claims to space | Autonomous crews, elected spokespeople; dispute legitimate targets and redistribution | Free ports, sympathetic suppliers and seizures; mobile decentralized wings | Rhizome sympathizers and critics; anti-monopoly raids, fugitive transport, diplomacy | Bright personalized liveries, blue and orange |
| The Gilded Corsairs | Privateering traditions and commercial raiders | Captains' compact and prize rules; opportunists test the code | Ransom, captured freight, private contracts; disciplined interception and withdrawal | Albion houses or corporations may employ them; negotiate surrender, privateer, escort or recover cargo | Gold, dark green, elegant hulls |
| The Veil | Distributed smuggling and intelligence network | Brokers and compartmentalized cells; disputes over secrecy and loyalty | Illicit freight, information, hidden ports; stealth and avoidance | Cross-border clients; covert transport, route discovery and intelligence work | Violet, charcoal, discreet markings |
| The Rustborn | Displaced workers and refugees living in mobile settlements | Ship councils and flotilla elders; survivalists debate raiding versus lawful salvage | Salvage, repair, barter and opportunistic theft; improvised ships, mobile support | UIU repair networks and humanitarian contacts; recover wrecks, rescue, trade or defend flotillas | Rust, patchwork panels, colorful repairs |

The Broken Standard may include veterans of the great war, possibly from multiple governments, who rejected disarmament or the peace settlement. This connection is proposed, not established; original allegiance and founding circumstances remain open (D6B §7).

### 7.1 Behavioral contrasts

Maw commanders may destroy cargo they could have captured; Corsairs prefer profitable surrender; Veil brokers prioritize avoiding attention; Rustborn captains weigh survival needs; Unbound cells consider ideological target legitimacy; Broken Standard officers weigh strategic objectives. These are doctrine preferences, not immutable scripts. Membership and promotions are organization-specific, and legal consequences depend on attributable behavior. A shortage can cause a pirate fleet to relocate, negotiate, raid, disband or seek new patrons instead of spawning replacements indefinitely.

## 8. Universal Academy of Sciences (UAS)

The Academy is a universe-wide institution attempting neutrality. Proposed founding or charter expansion: scholars organized cross-border cooperation in response to restrictions on exchange. The Academy may have prewar roots; the devastating war may have strengthened its mandate. Exact founding dates and treaty jurisdiction remain open (D6B §6). An elected scientific council, regional institutes and research ethics panels oversee programs. Open-science advocates, security-conscious researchers and commercial collaborators disagree about publication, access and funding. The Academy is not exempt from local laws or dependence on donors. Its installations are not offensive military bases; escorts and defensive capabilities are possible under agreements.

| Division | Activities and missions |
|---|---|
| Astronomical Sciences | Stellar surveys, observational equipment, unusual emissions |
| Xenobiology | Investigate possible extraterrestrial life, collect samples under appropriate protocols |
| Exogeology | Mineral and gas surveys, resource assessment |
| Archaeological Sciences | Investigate ruins and derelicts, provenance and recovery |
| Applied Physics | Anomalies, experiments, energy phenomena |
| Engineering Sciences | Test drives, instruments and components |
| Social Sciences | Cultural records, political and economic research |
| Environmental Sciences | Contamination, habitat and ecosystem monitoring |
| Cartographic Sciences | Map routes, hazards and unexplored locations |

Progression proposal: field associate → research assistant → accredited explorer → research fellow → senior fellow → principal investigator → Academy fellow → council member. Scientific authority governs research assignments and expeditions, not automatic military command. Assets include survey vessels, observatories, laboratories, archives and contracted escorts; dependencies include specialist instruments, samples, funding and host access. Visual identity: violet, white, cool blue; distinctive sensor arrays and luminous observatories. A contested discovery can generate competing research, patent, preservation, military and diplomatic objectives. Publish, delay or restrict access only through explicit policy and decision records.

## 10. Secular ideological movements

Movements are proposed cross-border networks, not necessarily centrally owned fleets. They can influence elections, policy, donations, protests, boycotts and recruitment. Their effects should be tied to actual membership, local support, organization capacity and legal context.

| Movement | Motivation and origin proposal | Internal disagreement | Potential activity / player involvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open Knowledge Movement | Reaction to exclusive patents and restricted archives | Unrestricted disclosure versus safety and consent | Open standards, publish research, recover records, negotiate licenses |
| Sovereign Individualists | Opposition to distant authorities imposing local rules | Individual rights versus shared obligations | Autonomy campaigns, charter negotiations, independent settlement support |
| Continuity Initiative | Response to civilization-threatening disasters | Centralized reserves versus distributed resilience | Emergency depots, archives, preparedness surveys, relief logistics |
| Synthetic Rights Coalition | Debate over legal status of artificial minds | Incremental protections versus full recognition | Legal advocacy, evidence transport, mediation; legal recognition, prevalence and nature of synthetic persons remain open |
| Expansionist Society | Belief that expansion secures opportunity and survival | Rapid expansion versus sustainable settlement | Survey expeditions, frontier investment, diplomatic access |
| Preservation League | Reaction to loss of historic sites and cultural records | Absolute preservation versus adaptive reuse | Heritage inventories, salvage mediation, protected-site campaigns |

Leadership could range from local organizers to elected delegates or funded spokespeople. Their visual identity should emerge through signage, media, emblems and civilian presence rather than requiring a unique fleet for every movement.

## 11. Neutral authorities

Neutral institutions serve participating jurisdictions through agreements. Their mandates may have been established or strengthened by the historical war; prewar roots remain possible (D6B §6). A service may be unavailable, contested, corrupt or denied recognition. Their authority is limited to their mandates; none is a universal sovereign.

| Authority | Mandate and governance proposal | Assets and dependencies | Player-facing functions and tensions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interstellar Transit Authority (ITA) | Treaty board coordinates public navigation standards, lane registration and hazard notices | Beacons, survey ships, control centers; host access and service funding | Charts, route surveys, repairs, diversions; local governments and contractors police routes; military and covert routes need not be public |
| Interstellar Arbitration Commission (IAC) | Delegates and independent arbiters mediate treaty, commercial and jurisdictional disputes | Offices, diplomatic transports, records; consent of parties | Negotiation, evidence delivery, ceasefire monitoring, prisoner exchanges; rulings require recognition and enforcement |
| Interstellar Rescue Service (IRS) | Independent emergency coordination and triage under humanitarian agreements | Rescue ships, hospital ships, depots; finite crews, fuel, donations and access | Search and rescue, evacuation, emergency supplies; competing emergencies and dangerous access |
| Universal Registry and Clearinghouse (URC) | Member-supervised registration, ownership and commercial settlement standards | Registry infrastructure and offices; trusted records, participating institutions | Ship transfers, investment records, contract settlement; disputed claims and nonrecognition; no universal surveillance |
| Bounty and Claims Commission (BCC) | Verifies recognized bounty and salvage claims and professional credentials | Records, adjudication offices, contracted investigators; evidence and jurisdictional cooperation | Bounty hunting, salvage disputes, missing ships; a warrant does not automatically authorize action everywhere |
| Interstellar Accreditation Bureau (IAB) | Voluntary engineering, safety and professional standards governed by technical panels | Test facilities, inspectors, reference standards; recognized certification agreements | Equipment tests, qualifications, tuning-stamp compliance; conflicts between safety, experimentation and proprietary influence |

Proposed institutional career pattern: registered associate → accredited practitioner → field coordinator → regional officer, with scope defined by mandate. Each has distinct service priorities rather than military rank by default. Visual language proposals: ITA blue/white navigation symbols; IAC neutral ivory/blue; IRS white/orange; URC teal/slate; BCC amber/gray; IAB clean cyan/white. All six are optional detailed institutions in early releases; a simple service abstraction may precede autonomous organizations.

## 12. Cross-organizational relationships

Relationships are directional and multidimensional: diplomatic recognition, trade access and dependency, military status, ideological affinity, historical grievances, trust and intelligence. The following are candidate starting pressures, not permanent alliances or confirmed initial conditions.

| The Concordancy / Bastion | Security cooperation, civil-liberty disputes | Joint patrol agreement followed by contested inspection rules |
| Albion / Choral | Ideological opposition, possible commodity trade | Disputed labor rights at a concession lead to arbitration or sanctions |
| Codex / Academy | Scientific cooperation, disagreement over restricted findings | Evidence of flawed prediction prompts inquiry and policy change |
| Gold League / IMU | Commercial dependence, bargaining conflict | Strike raises freight and material costs; alternative sourcing creates opportunities |
| Rhizome / Unbound | Shared autonomy ideals, disagreement over raids | A member habitat grants refuge while another condemns attacks |

The simulation should record why policies and relationships change. A faction may trade with an adversary under limited exceptions or continue honoring a rescue agreement during war. Individual membership, criminal liability and institutional hostility are distinct.

## 13. Player authority and institutional careers

Rank is not wealth, reputation, qualification or ownership. A faction-issued commission grants only delegated authority over assets within its jurisdiction and assignment. A private fleet may have multiple capital ships regardless of military rank, but owning those ships does not grant command over government forces. Qualifications can constrain appointment; reputation can affect recruitment and promotion without automatically granting permission.

| Authority stage | Military example | Civilian or institutional counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| Participant | Join Bomber Wing Delta against frigate Nevermind | Deliver assigned shipment or collect a sample |
| Formation leader | Select formation and maneuver | Coordinate a small survey team or convoy |
| Tactical commander | Assign authorized targets to wings | Reassign shipments or research tasks |
| Operational commander | Coordinate multiple formations and capital ships | Direct an expedition or regional transport operation |
| Strategic commander | Select campaign objectives and allocate authorized forces | Set research priorities or negotiate institutional strategy |

Exact titles, requirements, promotion and demotion rules are deferred. Command scope must reflect both rank and appointment; a senior officer cannot automatically seize unrelated fleets. Missions use authored templates populated from real available entities, needs and objectives. An NPC can fulfill an assignment without the player. Rank changes available decisions, not physics or damage bonuses. Retain cockpit and bridge piloting as the primary experience; delegation handles routine execution. Industrial investment is allowed, direct station and factory construction is excluded.

## 14. Logistics and institutional causality

Civilian, commercial and military transport connects actual suppliers and consumers. Routes can be publicly charted, observed, secret or disputed. Established commercial lanes generally receive heavier policing, but security depends on allocated patrols, local policy and current threats. Cargo must have an origin, destination, accountable quantity and transport state. A remote shipment can be represented abstractly without losing its identity or outcome; detailed local simulation must reconcile with that state. The simulation should avoid double-counting cargo, ships and events when changing detail levels.

### 14.2 Candidate event chains and mission hooks

| Trigger | Causal propagation | Potential assignments |
|---|---|---|
| IMU strike | Reduced extraction or delivery → inventory drawdown → price and production changes → alternative procurement | Mediate dispute, haul replacement ore, defend striking miners |
| Military convoy loss | Reduced fuel/parts → lower operational readiness → postpone or revise offensive | Escort replacement convoy, recover cargo, reconnaissance, negotiate access |
| Carrier destroyed | Actual fleet losses → changed force balance → strategic reassessment → possible territorial change | Join bomber wing, rescue survivors, cover withdrawal, blockade or defend station |
| Corporate patent dispute | Restricted parts or contested license → repair delays → rival production and legal actions | Deliver evidence, acquire authorized components, escort prototypes |
| Discovery of rare resource | Survey result → competing claims → investment, permits or conservation pressure | Survey, defend claim, negotiate terms, transport samples |
| Civilian patrol redeployment | Fewer escorts → route exposure → raids and higher insurance or alternative routes | Escort, bounty hunt, reroute freight, restore beacons |
| Station emergency | Supply and evacuation demand → rescue allocation → diversion of other services | Deliver medicine, evacuate personnel, recover stranded ships |
| Contested election or succession | Leadership change → revised policies and budgets → altered diplomatic and military orders | Transport delegates, verify evidence, protect or withdraw assets |

These chains are conditional, not guaranteed story beats. The player can intervene, abstain or pursue an unrelated career. Public reports should distinguish verified events from rumors, and hostile actors should act on obtainable intelligence rather than omniscient player tracking.

## 15. Content authoring and visual identity

Use a stable faction identity distinct from its display name. Names, insignia, ship operator markings, doctrine, rank terminology and institution descriptions are content; runtime assets, leadership, relationships and objectives are mutable world state. Future D9 should specify versioning and validation; D6A does not select a file format or engine dependency.

Recommended authoring profile: stable ID; display name and aliases; category; charter; historical summary; governance; memberships and rank roles; internal currents; laws and jurisdiction; assets and supply dependencies; strategic preferences; relationships; mission affinities; visual palette and terminology; content provenance; canon status. For movements without fleets, omit irrelevant assets and command fields rather than inventing them.

Visual cues should remain readable without color alone. Manufacturer shape language and operator liveries are separate. Vibrant environments, distinct station architecture, communications and iconography communicate identity without requiring a unique mesh for every organization. All art and insignia require original or appropriately licensed assets.

## 16. Scope and phased activation

The roster contains 38 concepts (7 governments, 4 megacorporations, 2 unions, 6 pirate factions, 1 academy, 6 religions, 6 ideological movements, 6 neutral authorities). This is a lore catalog, not a commitment to simulate 38 organizations at release. The D0 density-over-diameter principle governs implementation. Candidate responsibilities: strategic government/corporate actors; operational union/pirate actors; institutional research and services; social movements and belief networks. These categories can overlap and evolve.

First causal sandbox proposal: two systems, two contrasting governments (e.g. The Concordancy and Bastion), two competing corporate operators, an IMU supplier, a UIU repair facility, a pirate threat and lightweight transit/rescue services. One production chain, one civilian route, one military shipment and a small fleet operation are sufficient to demonstrate meaningful interactions. This selection is not a decision about canonical borders, permanent enemies or the initial playable region. Expand only when added institutions produce distinct gameplay and measured performance remains acceptable. A large-battle benchmark belongs to D10, not this lore document.

## 17. Design acceptance and open questions

Review criteria for D6A (not implementation acceptance): all seven canonical government names preserved; each government differs in legitimacy, policy, economy and response; corporations diversified across industries; both unions independent; pirates culturally distinct; Academy and neutral institutions have bounded authority; overlapping identities supported; persistent transport and rank-based command integrated; no requirement for empire construction; new lore explicitly marked proposed.

Future implementation evidence to define in D6–D10: same disturbance produces distinguishable institutional responses; cargo loss changes accountable inventories; absence of player does not freeze conflicts; permission checks prevent unauthorized orders; real battle participants bind to mission templates and destroyed targets invalidate or resolve assignments; saved/reloaded world preserves relevant assets, claims, memberships, ranks and shipments; detail transitions do not duplicate resources. These are candidate acceptance scenarios, not executed tests.

Open for owner review:

- Which historical eras and founding stories become canon? Which calendar, arrival sequence and detailed expedition histories should be adopted within the established Earth-origin setting?
- Which governments, corporations and smaller organizations should be active in the first region? Which initial rivalries are desired?
- What forms of citizenship, multiple membership, defection and conflicting obligations are allowed?
- What forms of nonhuman life, precursor remains and synthetic consciousness should be encountered? Preserve the established absence of known contemporary extraterrestrial civilizations; synthetic personhood and its legal status remain open.
- How far should government change, corporate collapse, faction secession and territorial conquest proceed?
- Which laws create enjoyable gameplay without excessive administrative friction?
- What extent of strategic authority should a player attain, and how is it delegated without displacing piloting?
- Which institutional services are universal gameplay abstractions versus fully simulated organizations?
