## 1. Purpose

Record enough setting history to support gameplay, exploration, faction identity and content generation. Further fiction is deferred until it serves implementation or content needs. This is not a technical specification or a mandatory story campaign. The setting is independently authored for Interstellar Meridian. This migration does not add historical canon, approve candidate fiction, or establish a technical implementation milestone.

## 2. Established setting

- Humanity originated on Earth, which was left behind long ago. The game takes place in another galaxy; Earth is not an ordinary accessible destination.
- Travel across the adopted galaxy is established. Intergalactic travel requires generation ships or an extraordinary expenditure of energy. Exact propulsion and travel times remain open.
- Contemporary civilizations are exclusively of human origin (established setting premise). Other lifeforms exist, and no contemporary extraterrestrial civilizations are known. The latter describes contemporary knowledge; it does not authorize changing the established premise. Artifacts survive from civilizations that destroyed themselves or otherwise disappeared.
- Artificial intelligence, cybernetics, genetic engineering and synthetic consciousness are setting topics accepted in principle; prevalence, limits, legal status and mechanics remain open. These are not a commitment to implement every technology or a separate nonhuman civilization.
- Seven governments descend from distinct Earth-origin generation-ship expeditions launched at different times by different Earth factions. Arrival order, voyage durations, ship names and precise Earth origins are not fixed.
- A vast war devastated settled space. The governments now largely coexist in a fragile peace; some are friendly to one another. The war helps explain the need for neutral institutions and the Academy's cross-border role.
- The setting has a 5,000-year history. Working interpretation: approximately 5,000 years since human settlement began in the adopted galaxy, rather than the physical age of the galaxy. This interpretation and calendar epoch remain subject to confirmation.

## 3. The seven expeditions

Names and political concepts are established; the specific expedition origins below are proposals except for Bastion's emergency.

| Civilization | Established political identity | Proposed expedition heritage |
|---|---|---|
| The Concordancy | Constitutional representative democracy | Coalition of democratic nations and international institutions; preserved charter |
| Albion | Neo-feudal aristocracy | Privately financed expedition; hereditary stewardship emerged from shipboard custodianship |
| Codex | Algorithmic technocracy | Scientific and engineering coalition; evidence-based resource allocation |
| The Gold League | Contractual shareholder government | Commercial consortium; passage, duties and rights governed through contracts |
| Choral | Decentralized cooperative socialism | Labor, agricultural and ecological cooperatives; shared essential resources |
| Bastion | Militarized security state | Premature partial awakening following onboard emergency; see §4 |
| The Rhizome | Confederation of autonomous settlements | Alliance of independent communities; modular autonomous habitats |

The founding vessels may survive as monuments, habitats, archives, citadels, wrecks or dismantled infrastructure. Their current fates are not fixed. This offers future location design opportunities without requiring them for the first playable region.

## 4. Bastion and the Awakened

**Established:** An onboard emergency during the voyage prematurely awakened part of the cryogenically suspended population. Repairs were necessary, many cryogenic pods were lost, and the Awakened fought over limited supplies. This experience shaped Bastion.

**Proposed elaboration:** The emergency exposed limited life-support, food and replacement capacity. Conflicting priorities over repairs and protecting sleepers led to internal conflict and eventually centralized emergency authority. Modern preparedness, public service, reserves, redundancy and vigilance may descend from these conditions. The extent of casualties, exact failure, factions involved, event names and duration are deliberately unspecified. Possible internal tension: whether permanent emergency institutions remain justified long after arrival.

## 5. Alien life and archaeology

Life beyond humanity provides discovery opportunities without requiring active alien political factions. Candidate content includes microbial samples, complex ecosystems, unusual atmospheric or spaceborne organisms, ruined installations, derelict vessels, dormant machines and data archives. Spaceborne organisms and the existence of multiple unrelated precursor cultures are proposals, not settled facts.

Earlier working archaeological labels (optional): Architects (monumental structures), Lattice (crystalline machines), Hollow Ones (excavated bodies), Silent (ruins and communications), Wanderers (derelict ships). Do not assume these are confirmed distinct species, or that all disappearances share a cause. No explanation is locked.

Discoveries may support research, trade, salvage, diplomacy, resource claims and specialized component parts; some are valuable for knowledge alone. Ancient artifacts do not automatically grant powerful technology. Human wrecks and historical sites are equally valid exploration content.

## 6. War and fragile peace

The war's name, causes, alliances, duration, casualties and settlement date remain open. Working names: **Great Fracture** for the war and **Interstellar Accords** for the postwar agreements.

Candidate consequences: contested territory, damaged industry, displaced populations, abandoned colonies, surplus military vessels, lost records, disrupted shipping, unresolved grievances, and reconstruction demand. These are useful sources of actual world conditions and missions, not mandatory scripted events.

The neutral authorities and Universal Academy of Sciences can have prewar roots; the war may have established or strengthened their cross-border mandates. The Transit Authority coordinates navigation, Arbitration Commission mediates disputes, Rescue Service provides emergency response, Registry and Clearinghouse supports ownership and trade, Bounty and Claims Commission coordinates recognized claims, Accreditation Bureau supports standards, and Academy supports scientific cooperation. Exact founding dates and treaty jurisdiction are open.

## 7. The Broken Standard

Retained D6A concept: a renegade military organization composed of former naval personnel, retaining formal ranks, disciplined fleets and strategic ambitions. Some seek revenge; others sovereignty. They are not simply indiscriminate pirates.

Proposed connection: veterans of the great war, possibly from multiple governments, who rejected disarmament or the peace settlement. Original allegiance and founding circumstances remain unresolved; the owner asked for a reminder, not approval of a new origin.

## 8. Optional chronology (not canon)

Earlier brainstorming suggested Settlement Year (SY), with the first arrival as SY 0 and the present as SY 5000. Candidate eras: Exodus before SY 0; First Settlements 0–600; Founding 600–1400; Expansion 1400–2600; Consolidation 2600–3800; Rivalry 3800–4750; Great Fracture 4750–4960; Accords 4960–5000. The dates, calendar name, war duration and 40-year peace are provisional and should not constrain simulation or content. Galaxy-wide travel capability is not a requirement to populate or simulate an entire galaxy at full detail.

## 9. Gameplay handoff

| Setting premise | Gameplay implication |
|---|---|
| Distinct founding expeditions | Recognizable government doctrine, culture and location identity |
| Interstellar travel, isolated galaxy | Local and intersystem navigation; no intergalactic travel needed |
| Fragile peace | Diplomacy, trade, reconstruction, military service and possible renewed conflicts |
| Ancient and human remains | Discoverable sites, research, salvage and rare parts |
| Neutral institutions | Cross-border contracts, scientific careers, rescue and arbitration |
| Persistent history | Record meaningful changes in assets, territory, logistics and relationships during play |

No mandatory storyline, planetary walking, alien empire simulation, detailed historical chronology or comprehensive speculative technology tree is required by this checkpoint.

## 10. Documentation reconciliation

D6A v0.2.1 §3 and the individual government profiles reconcile earlier provisional founding traditions with the established generation-ship origins and Bastion's voyage emergency. Compatible earlier traditions may describe later political development, not alternative origins of humanity or the expeditions. D6A's roster and organizational concepts remain intact. D6A's detailed institutions, founding narratives, relationships and ranks retain their proposed status unless explicitly established by the owner.

D0 v1.1.1 sets the vision; D1 v2.1.1 carries the high-level requirements. D6B's setting premises are constraints on content, not instructions to implement all speculative technologies, organizations or historical eras. D3 will establish geography and travel assumptions; D5 will refine equipment and flight; D6 and D7 will refine political, economic and agent behavior; D8 will specify mission binding; D9 will establish content and persistence contracts; D10 will govern implementation gates. Historical dates and candidate labels must not be treated as stable technical identifiers or simulation parameters without a subsequent decision.

The game title does not establish an in-universe navigation reference, institution or historical event. Meridian Consolidated remains a proposed corporate name in D6A v0.2.1; no change is made here.
